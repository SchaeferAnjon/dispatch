# Item-by-item checklist for one topic

In the order students learn. The brackets after each item give the levels it applies to: H = high priority (must master), M = medium (understanding is enough), L = low (a look is enough). The level names and proportions follow the project's `TEACHING.md`.

## A. Path and order (learning mode)

- [ ] A first-time learner has a path of one step per screen (learning mode); the reference page is a different use. (H, M)
- [ ] Screen 1 is a question one can guess at, picking up the question the previous topic left open. (H, M)
- [ ] Within the first 2 screens the student does or answers something once. (H, M)
- [ ] Formulas, terms and analogies all appear after the screen where the student needs them. (all)
- [ ] Calculations in cases or worked problems come no earlier than the screen that teaches the calculation. (H, M)
- [ ] At least one retrieval every 3 to 4 screens: a question, a calculation step or a one-sentence retelling. (H, M)
- [ ] One thing per screen: no more than 3 sentences of explanation, only one reading highlighted. (H, M)
- [ ] The last screen has a one-sentence landing point and the opening question of the next topic. (H, M)

## B. Opening and main line

- [ ] The opening question can be answered in 30 seconds, offers 2 to 4 clickable guesses, and is not answered right after guessing. (H, M)
- [ ] A lesson has one big question, and every topic takes a step toward it. (whole lesson)
- [ ] Topics are joined by transitions: "the last one solved X, but brought Y", not a recap like "the last topic covered X". (all)
- [ ] Conclusions cited have already been taught; when a later conclusion is needed, move it forward or give a one-sentence conclusion with a link. (all)

## C. Examples and analogies

- [ ] The lesson's running example is used, with real numbers that can be calculated by hand all the way through. (H, M)
- [ ] Cases have sources; at most one made-up model per page, with its assumptions stated first. (H, M)
- [ ] Analogies have three lines: the mapping (what corresponds to what), where it breaks, what the break leads to. (all)
- [ ] One analogy scene per lesson where possible, with fixed roles; draw the analogy on the figure where possible. (whole lesson)

## D. Figures, animations, steps

- [ ] Symbols in figures have the same names and colours as in the text; numbers are written on the figure; captions sit right next to it. (all with figures)
- [ ] Every figure has one sentence on "look here first". (all with figures)
- [ ] Animations show only the topic itself; they can be stepped and paused; the task is written as "guess first … then drag …". (H, M)
- [ ] Step-by-step explanations give one conclusion per step, and steps that can be seen in the animation switch it to the matching state. (H, M)
- [ ] Key states also come as small static multiples, to take in at a glance when reviewing. (H)

## E. Common mistakes and questions

- [ ] Common mistakes: 2 to 4 for H, 1 to 2 for M, written as "you might think … in fact … tell them apart at a glance by …", preferably from real questions. (H, M)
- [ ] Quizzes: at least one asks why; distractors are real misconceptions, not obvious throwaways; the correct answer's position varies; each question has an explanation, ideally one sentence for each wrong option too. (H, M; at most one for L)
- [ ] Calculation questions are graded: full worked example → one or two steps left blank → independent → variations; no questions with zero information (like "what is the penalty when λ = 0"). (H)
- [ ] Hints teach estimating the order of magnitude ("the answer should be between 0.1 and 10") and calculator brackets. (with calculations)
- [ ] A lesson ends with a set of mixed practice. (whole lesson)

## F. Short answers in the exam's language

- [ ] Questions are phrased like the sample exam (command words, marks, whether a drawing is required). (H, at most one for M)
- [ ] There is an answer skeleton (by command word: one structure each for define, explain, compare, draw, calculate). (H)
- [ ] There is a low-mark example with why it lost marks. (at least one for H)
- [ ] Marking points have one mark each; writing about 60% of them earns most of the marks. (H)
- [ ] Model answer length follows the marks (about 1 mark per minute of writing). (H)
- [ ] Drawing questions accept a photo of a hand drawing for marking; key terms can be practised for spelling (when the exam is handwritten). (H)

## G. Feynman and review

- [ ] Feynman points are concrete down to formulas, numbers and conditions, covering every sentence of "on this page you only need to know". (H)
- [ ] The beginner asks with a common misconception at least once. (H)
- [ ] After explaining, a few sentences are written in the exam's language and marked against the marking points. (H)
- [ ] All H and M topics learned go into spaced review (not only failed ones); review units of 2 to 3 minutes. (H, M)
- [ ] Points not covered in the explanation automatically become review cards. (H)

## H. Levels and length

- [ ] The levels match the project's level data; every sentence of "on this page you only need to know" can be found in the explanation and the questions. (all)
- [ ] Lengths differ visibly: low priority is read in one screen; medium in two or three; high priority is a full lesson. (all)
- [ ] Medium priority is not so thin it cannot be understood: why, an analogy, an example with numbers, a one-sentence summary and questions are all there. (M)
- [ ] Folding or "key points mode" keeps the most important figure and the one-sentence analogy and folds away long text. (M, L)

## I. The original, terms, formulas

- [ ] Checked against the slides or textbook page, with labels matching the original; what the original drew is not labelled "extra reading". (all)
- [ ] Terms appear the first time with the original (for example the English term when the explanation is in Chinese and the exam in English), matching the slides' wording. (all)
- [ ] Every new symbol is in "new symbols on this page" with how to read it, what it means and a real number. (all)
- [ ] Formulas follow the original in one consistent typesetting (such as LaTeX), calculated once with numbers before they first appear. (with formulas)
- [ ] No more than 6 new terms per topic and 2 per paragraph. (all)

## J. Plain words (check every paragraph)

The general rule for every agent is Soul (`~/.dispatch/rules/SOUL.md`); this section is its finer version for lessons.

- [ ] The first sentence of each paragraph could serve as its title.
- [ ] Is there a sentence whose removal changes nothing? There must be none.
- [ ] Read aloud: no three sentences in a row of the same length; the conclusion is a short sentence.
- [ ] No flat lists of "every aspect", no "first, second, finally", no "on the one hand, on the other hand".
- [ ] No paragraph ends by summarizing again; one summary only, at the end of the page.
- [ ] No stock phrases: it is worth noting, all in all, let us take a look, it is not hard to see, simply put, in other words, crucial, plays an important role.
- [ ] No announcements ("we will now analyse three aspects"), no praise ("good question"), no closing invitations ("anything else you would like to ask?").
- [ ] Labels and hedges total no more than 3 per page, each marked once.
- [ ] No fixed patterns repeated across pages.
- [ ] Adjectives replaced by numbers ("540 times more expensive", not "very expensive").

## K. AI tutor answers (spot-check real conversations)

- [ ] It first judges the kind of sticking point (symbol, confusion, causation, figure, calculation, what the question wants) and answers by kind.
- [ ] It restates "are you asking X?" only when the question is unclear; clear questions get answered directly.
- [ ] Facts and symbols are answered directly without counter-questions; the Socratic style is only for practice questions.
- [ ] The same question asked a second time: give the answer directly with an example with numbers.
- [ ] When the answer is in a later chapter: give a one-sentence conclusion with a link, not only "the original does not cover this".
- [ ] Length as set (for example 3 to 6 sentences), one focus per paragraph, the closing question optional.
